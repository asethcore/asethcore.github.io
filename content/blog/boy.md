A new approach for this one: the approach is that instead of following the normal routine of writing, editing, and posting all within the span of a single day—or to be precise, in a matter of like 4–6 hours—I will be treating this blog as a sort of memoir, only writing stuff that I find related to each other. Or maybe just write whatever I want and then remove whatever I don't want to share.

These days (one of these days I will go in peace), I feel a wave of happiness and joy not related to anything in particular. Nothing has happened since my last blog, whenever that was, and I mean nothing at all really except the passing of days. I am not doubting anything about it; why should I? I am just glad it is like that, but I don't want it to last like this—don't want this moment to last forever, though of course the feeling is quite needed for me, or for anyone for that matter. I am not anticipating anything bad to happen in that sense; what I mean to say is that if anything unpleasant were to happen in the near future, I am certainly not worried about or thinking of it… What I am currently feeling, I would describe as the joy of the present (oh god, Lana is so good). Of course, there can also be sadness in the present, but it is just another possibility—a possibility that I am not living currently, though I won't deny it either. Why should I? You have to live through bad days to be truly grateful when you are living the good days, and hope for them when you are in your bad days (you know it). (Mr. Born to Lose, I like you a lot.)

There is this that I've been thinking about today: you know I don't have any sort of plan for how I want to live my upcoming decade. As a matter of fact, I never had any long-term plans, and in my case, I never had plans, let alone long-term ones. I just do things I want to; sometimes I just have to do them because apparently there comes a priority to some matters which can't be ignored, mostly when external entities are involved, and I feel neutral about it for the most part. But here is my ideal plan—the ideal thing I would want to do in the coming decade. I am thinking of, well, first completing my bachelor's degree in electronics and probably getting a job to return what I owe to the bank. Then go to some foreign university to study architecture, as it's another one of my interests—probably do a bachelor's and a master's, and I don't know, a career shift, I don't know. You know, I want to either work as a zookeeper or a teacher/professor—probably an easygoing one. Why these professions? Of course, I don't think that these jobs are easy in any way; it's just that I like sharing my knowledge with others for the teaching one, and for the zookeeper one, I like animals. They are like me: they can't express much when they say anything, but I think if they knew how to write, they would do the same messy job that I do in writing.

While I am at it, I have never mentioned why I don't write in my native language. Why English? Well, the translation thing is surely not one of the reasons. The reason is—well, let me explain it to you: I can't communicate properly in speech most of the time. I don't find the words, or let's say words don't get to me, or they don't want to reach me—I don't know, it's not mutual, by the way. So in order to replicate this same thing, I wager on English, as it's not my native tongue and I am not fluent in it.

Where was I? Yeah, the plan. Those were my desired professions. Onto how I might want to settle down or where I want to settle down: I don't think I want to settle down. I can't think of any reason to do so, or maybe whatever I am doing, I already feel settled. That would make your and my definition of settling down vastly different. My definition of settling down would be: if you are happy where you are, you are already settled, for it doesn't have to be constant. And what I assume your definition to be is like taking hold of some land, finding your LOL (love of life), and carrying on the cycle you lived. The cycle is not bad; it's just that I don't think I could ever find a LOML, and even if I did find one, I don't know how open they would be to this ideal thing of mine. And if it's great and they support me and continue to live with me, I still won't settle with your definition of settling down—I will just take her with me wherever I go. And if I would want to settle with your definition, I might settle down; what matters is the spur of the moment. The freedom I want is not about doing whatever comes to mind; it's about whether I can control myself—kinda ironic, ain't it? Freedom coming from control. I think I don't understand what I want to say; what I mean is happiness is what matters to me. I could be a fool and still be happy, and I won't give a shit about it. Fools don't give a shit. Now of course, this is not the plan for my coming decade, but it's what it ideally would be—different things. I am nothing but a dreamer after all; aren't we all the same? You see, freedom is not the only thing I care about, not even happiness; it's just that aside from all these feelings or state of mind—whatever you may call these—there's a feeling that sums it all up for you, and there are only two parts to it: you like that or you don't like that. If I like it, what's the problem? If I don't, well, I will certainly meet with a moment again and I would like that, and I would count this one worth it. That's how it goes for me.

I've come to realize that my fear of losing myself wasn't real at all. I was afraid that if I took certain actions, I'd be back to feeling drained out. I'd want to be alone not because I want to, but because I am so tired of my own mind that I would want to look for a switch to turn it off. But I realized this fear isn't real at all, because the "me" I am afraid of losing is not a separate thing I possess. The neat part is I don't possess it—it is me. I don't possess it, I *am* it, and I think losing it is not possible, or so I think. Anyways, I am just writing thoughts that I had tucked away in a corner, and they are collectively appearing as I write.

Okay, something aside from this: I've been spending my last month and the current month pretending to be a cinephile—pretending to myself. Well, I am kind of trying to understand how directors try to keep their movies interesting and how they can manage to pull it all through in the span of a standard movie. I have two favorite movies that were released in the past month: one is *Resident Evil* and the other is *Primetime*. Now, the way Zach Cregger did his version of the movie... well, first I would like to state something that I feel is true for this genre: there isn't much to it, it's the same plot almost all the time. There is a zombie virus and we know the protagonist of the movie is surely going to die—of course, what else can he do? So he played with the environment and the camera work, and well, he tried to capture what the real condition of a common guy would be if such an outbreak were to happen. Not everyone is Joel or Ethan Winters. So yeah, the movie tries to be serious most of the time, which it succeeds in doing, and when there is humor, it actually works because it's planned—it's not that the scene is trying to be serious and turns out hilarious. Onto *Primetime*, I would say Robert's acting delivery and the script itself are what made it work for me. The aspect ratio was not something that was needed, but I think this movie is good at keeping track of the central plot and not becoming a complete biography of the character rather than that of the show. I also watched 8 other films, but I ain't going to share my observations on them—it would take too long. For this month, I am waiting for *Social Reckoning*.

To be truly honest, my social life would be much smaller or maybe non-existent if it wasn't for all the people who go out with me, talk to me, and ask me to do things. I kinda owe them this, you know? Most of the time in my everyday life, the only time I step outside the house is when my friends call me to hang out or when I have to go outside for university. I mostly avoid going outside; even if I have to buy something from the store, I ask my brother to do so because I find it difficult, or rather a little challenging. This is not a matter of laziness, and I don't dismiss it as my laziness—this is the truth: I am shy, very shy. If people didn't want to be friends with me or if they hadn't initiated convos with me, I wouldn't have many friends or any at all. I might as well be a Dracula who doesn't even come out at night. "Human is a social animal"—well, this human isn't, and I am trying to change it. Somewhere in between all this, I somehow lost the courage to tell things even to my parents, like my course of action. I rarely share myself in speech not because i cant but because i dont find the information needed, so not many people know what I am on the inside. I have an upcoming plan of going out with my friend(s); I wouldn't inform my parents about this until the last moment. This is not because I don't want to tell them, but because I still haven't figured out how to deliver it to them. I am just too involved with this that I usually don't care how they will react to this information, its mostly the initiating part where i get hanged up a bit but once i am past that i can be a chatterbox.
