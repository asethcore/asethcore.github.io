### Kafka on the Shore | Haruki Murakami {#kafka-on-the-shore}

{% book_meta(title="Kafka on the Shore | Haruki Murakami", rating=5, pages=467, genre="fiction", finished="2025-00-00") %}

ahhh this one had me impressed the way the story evolves and ofcourse the absurd erotic moments at this point it just makes me laugh how this plays out in murakami's novels but story wise it truly is a very well written novel especially the forest part.

>Sometimes fate is like a small sandstorm that keeps changing directions. You change direction but the sandstorm chases you. You turn again, but the storm adjusts. Over and over you play this out, like some ominous dance with death just before dawn. Why? Because this storm isn't something that blew in from far away, something that has nothing to do with you. This storm is you. Something inside of you. So all you can do is give in to it, step right inside the storm, closing your eyes and plugging up your ears so the sand doesn't get in, and walk through it, step by step. There's no sun there, no moon, no direction, no sense of time. Just fine white sand swirling up into the sky like pulverized bones. That's the kind of sandstorm you need to imagine.

>And you really will have to make it through that violent, metaphysical, symbolic storm. No matter how metaphysical or symbolic it might be, make no mistake about it: it will cut through flesh like a thousand razor blades. People will bleed there, and you will bleed too. Hot, red blood. You'll catch that blood in your hands, your own blood and the blood of others.

>And once the storm is over you won't remember how you made it through, how you managed to survive. You won't even be sure, in fact, whether the storm is really over. But one thing is certain. When you come out of the storm you won't be the same person who walked in. That's what this storm's all about.

{% end %}

### Norwegian Wood | Haruki Murakami {#norwegian-wood}

{% book_meta(title="Norwegian Wood | Haruki Murakami", rating=4, pages=296, genre="literary fiction", finished="2024-00-00") %}

this being the first ever novel that i read id say it was a pretty good start, no one recommended this to me, not even the author was recommended. i just went to the book stall and picked this one up. i am writing this review after reading more of his stuff and i would say that this book leans a little back on the fiction part i mean the story is still fictional but you wont read the type of stuff he has in his other books, im not complaining about it, every book is supposed to carry a different theme. after reading what people think of murakami's work as some sort of male glazy, id like to lean back on that i mean it has what people say it has but almost all of murakami's work has a self narrative and when a man is narrating himself what do you expect? i think this has turned more into a author review than a book review but i am still pretty new at reviewing things considering this is my first.

> death is not opposite of life, its a part of life.
